# Hyde Park Avenue Action - Design Brief

## Objective

Help a Forest Hills resident understand, in under a minute, that Boston's August 2026 resurfacing plan omits stronger safety elements shown in earlier plans - then make it easy to send a personal email to the Chief of Streets.

## Primary audience

People who live, walk, bike, ride the bus, drive, shop, or attend school near Hyde Park Avenue. They may know the street well without knowing transportation-design terminology or the history of the planning process.

## Core story

1. The City is about to resurface Hyde Park Avenue.
2. The current plan does not adequately address longstanding pedestrian-safety concerns.
3. Earlier City concepts demonstrate that more substantial changes were considered.
4. Residents can still ask the City to use this moment to build a safer design.

## Experience principles

- Action before organization: the problem, comparison, and email action come before background material.
- Understandable at a glance: use plain-language labels, a four-plan switcher, and short feature lists.
- Neighbor-led, not agency-branded: confident editorial typography, real local imagery, direct language, and visible independence from the City.
- Urgent but credible: make the stakes clear without overstating what the source drawings prove.
- One dominant action: "Write the City" remains visible throughout the page.

## Visual direction

The site should feel like a handsome neighborhood campaign poster expanded into a web page. Use deep green, warm paper, safety yellow, and one urgent red. Combine large serif headlines with compact sans-serif explanatory text. Let the actual plan drawings carry the evidence. Avoid decorative illustrations, excessive animation, institutional blue, and portfolio-style case-study language.

## Page structure

1. Hero: local aerial photograph, one-sentence problem, two routes forward - compare or act.
2. Plan comparison: August 2026 selected by default, with one-click access to both May 2025 alternatives and the August 2020 redesign. Each view shows the source image, what it includes, and what was lost or limited.
3. Action: an editable letter addressed to the current Chief of Streets, with an email-app handoff and a copy option.
4. Sources and independence note.

## Interaction and accessibility

- Plan choices behave as accessible tabs and support arrow-key navigation.
- Plan drawings open in a large, dismissible view and remain available as original PDFs.
- The persistent action button becomes a full-width mobile control.
- Strong contrast, large touch targets, visible keyboard focus, semantic labels, and reduced-motion support are required.

## Tone

Direct, neighborly, specific, and constructive. The campaign should sound firm about the failure of the current plan while remaining focused on a practical request: use resurfacing to install meaningful, durable safety improvements.

## First-version success criteria

- A new visitor can state the problem after viewing the first screen.
- The current plan and three prior alternatives are reachable with one click each.
- The main differences are understandable without reading the drawings.
- A resident can personalize and open the letter in their email app in under two minutes.
